Solution explanation:
Question 1: The point here is that you are working with qubits. By definition they are objects of dimension 2. The [1,0] is telling you that the probability amplitude for being in the first basis state is 1, and the probability amplitude for being in the second basis state is 0. These two basis states are labelled '0' and '1' respectively. Question 2: The {'0': 1024} means "when I measure the state, if I repeat the measurement 1024 times, in this case I get the answer '0' for all 1024 of those trials. So, it's basically an experimental estimate of the probability of an outcome, which should correspond to the mod-square of the probability amplitude for that basis element. Question 3: yes. I'm not a qiskit expert, but I think it would be sufficient to call to get two qubits. The second number is the number of classical bits that you want. Question 4: Your computation has to start from somewhere. It needs to be a fixed, known, state. By convention it's the $|0\rangle$ state. It's just a matter of labelling. Whatever state is easiest to prepare, that's the only we call '0'.
