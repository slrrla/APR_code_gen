Solution explanation:
Superposition states of a qubit are usually of form $$| \psi \rangle = a |0\rangle + b |1\rangle $$ where probabilities of getting state $|0\rangle$ and$ |1\rangle$ after a $Z$-measurement are $$p(0) = |a|^2$$ $$p(0) = |b|^2$$ Equivprobable states imply that the chances of getting any outcome are the same. i.e., for 1-qubit $$|a|^2 = |b|^2 = 0.5$$ or $$ a = b = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\,\, \text{(upto a global phase)}$$ For the code you have given, you are applying Hadamard on both qubits. (0) implies operation on qubit0 and (1) implies operation on qubit1. The resultant state, say $|\phi\rangle$ would be $ \begin{align} |\phi\rangle &= (H \otimes H) |00\rangle \\ &= \frac{1}{2}(|00\rangle + |01\rangle +|10\rangle+|11\rangle) \end{align} $ and thus, resulting in an equiprobable state where the probability of getting any of the four outcomes - $\{|00\rangle , |01\rangle ,|10\rangle,|11\rangle\}$ is equal, and it is $|\frac{1}{2}|^2 = 0.25$ An equviprobable state just means a superposition state with equal probabilities.
