Solution explanation:
The result of the transpilation you are looking at is the consequence of unitary synthesis, which runs as part of the transpilation process: There are many algorithm to convert a unitary matrix into gates. However, in general, they do not guarantee that conversion is optimal in terms of the target gates. They preserve the result, as you notice, but the "meaning" is lost. The gate have no specific meaning, they are two-qubits gates that the synthesis algorithm calculated. If you want to decompose that even further as rotations, run . In general, it is hard to "recover semantics" (the fact that matrix was describing an ) once you are lower in the abstraction (a matrix, in this case). If you want to preserve that, I recommend you to use less abstract constructors. In this case, :

```
from qiskit.transpiler.passes import UnitarySynthesis UnitarySynthesis()(circ).draw("mpl")
```
