Solution explanation:
The function only exposes the construction layer below of some given circuit or gate implementation. It has nothing to do with the basis gates of a particular backend. Although, in some given library, a lot of the gates might be constructed out of components of a popular gate-set. In any case, to transform a circuit to a basis set, you need to transpile it by giving the transpiler information about which gate set you want to use. Here’s an analogy using classical circuits. Imagine you have a library that has a circuit for a full adder. You decide to “decompose$^*$” the circuit and find out that it is made out of AND, OR and NOT gates. That’s all well and good, but it is not giving you information about the actual type transistors that will be used to implement that in a specific chip. You would then need to “transpile$^*$” your circuit to a specific technology node (let’s say TSMC CMOS 65 nm) to match the hardware where that circuit will be implemented. $^*$Decompose and transpile and not words typically used in digital circuit design. Just using them here for the purpose of this analogy. Answer to Edit1 You transpiled your circuit using as the backend. All of the gates shown in the circuit diagram you shared are part of the basis gates of the .
